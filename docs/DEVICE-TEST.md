# Device tests

The simulator decodes no video and AVPlayer shows no picture there, so playback is proven only on a real iPhone: iOS 27, unplugged, Low Power Mode off, against the home Plex server. The IDs in brackets point at the video engine's device test sheets (videoengine `docs/ENGINE.md`, "Device test sheets"), which list the exact clips.

Each check records three things:

- **Diagnostics:** in the player, More › Playback Info › Copy Diagnostics, pasted into the report. It carries the route and why, the stream, the engine's recent measurements (`perf-*`, `remux-*`, `hw-*` lines) and the phone's decoders.
- **Stats:** More › Stats for Nerds (or a long press on the title) shows the same lines live; its Copy button copies them at that moment.
- **Screenshots** where a check is about what the screen shows. HDR brightness does not survive a screenshot; describe it instead.

## Checks

1. **Direct play.** A movie in MP4 or MOV, H.264 or HEVC with AAC. Pass: stats Route "Direct Play" with no Why line; it plays and seeks to 10, 50 and 90 % without a stall. Note the seconds to the first picture. Paste Diagnostics.
2. **MKV remux, HEVC.** A 1080p HEVC MKV and a 4K HDR10 MKV, five cold starts each, then five far seeks and three near seeks, then ten minutes of the 4K one [P1–P3]. Pass: Route "Repackaged on the phone", Why "MKV file", Picture ends "hardware decode"; Remux lead stays above 0 s; Heat at most Fair; Frames shows no stalls. Paste Diagnostics after the ten minutes. On a Mac, Console with subsystem `VideoEngine` shows the start signposts [P4].
3. **DTS and TrueHD.** One MKV with DTS, one with TrueHD, ideally the one-tone-per-channel clips [T4]; then AC-3, E-AC-3 (Atmos on AirPods Pro), FLAC and AAC files [T1–T3, T5–T7]. Pass: DTS and TrueHD show Audio "… converted" and every tone comes from its own speaker; AC-3 and E-AC-3 show "copied"; lip sync holds. Paste Diagnostics for DTS and for TrueHD.
4. **HDR and Dolby Vision.** HDR10, HLG and HDR10+ files [F1–F4, F12], then Dolby Vision profiles 5, 7 and 8.1 [F5–F11]. Pass: Picture names the format; highlights brighten past SDR white; profile 7 plays as HDR10 (Why "Dolby Vision profile 7 plays as HDR10"); no black picture or green tint; no error. Paste Diagnostics for each Dolby Vision profile.
5. **Anime with a dub and subtitles.** An episode with Japanese and English audio, full English subtitles and an English dub transcript, and an intro and credits marker. Pass: the Tracks menu picks the audio from Settings › Audio & Subtitles; switching to the dub with Follow the Audio on picks the dub transcript; Skip Intro counts down 5 s then skips, and Watch Intro stops it; Up Next counts down 5 s at the credits, and Watch Credits stops it. Subtitles: cues on time after seeks, styled ASS keeps italics [S1–S3, S5, S8]. Screenshot the Skip Intro pill and the Up Next card.
6. **Picture subtitles.** A Blu-ray MKV with PGS (and a forced-only PGS track), and a DVD MKV with VobSub [S6, S7]. Pass: subtitles sit in the picture, not on the black bars, in both orientations; forced-only shows only the forced lines; pause, seek and 2× keep them right. Screenshot one PGS line.
7. **Picture in Picture.** During an MKV, swipe to the Home Screen with the player upright, return through the window's restore button, and repeat with the player on its side; then once paused, and once from the Picture in Picture button [S4]. Swipe Home also on a live channel read from the tuner and on one through Plex (Live TV › Direct from Tuner off). Pass, with Settings › General › Picture in Picture › Start PiP Automatically on: while it plays the picture moves into the window by itself and keeps playing; paused, no window opens and the sound stays off; text subtitles show in a film's window; picture subtitles are absent (expected); restoring brings back the full player at the same time, turned as Video Orientation says; a pause or play in a live window shows on the live player afterwards; closing a live window on the Home Screen frees the tuner. The swipe Home and the live windows are untested on hardware so far.
8. **Lock Screen.** Play an episode, then lock the iPhone. Pass: sound continues; the Lock Screen shows the poster, title and "S2 · E5 · Name"; play, pause, skip back and skip forward work and the time stays right after each.
9. **AirPlay.** AirPlay to an Apple TV, once from a direct-play file and once from an MKV [S9]. Pass: a tap anywhere on the AirPlay circle opens the system picker; the glyph turns accent once the TV plays; the direct-play file plays on the TV as it is; the MKV moves to the server within a few seconds at the same time (Route "Repackaged by the server" or "Converted by the server", Why "AirPlay needs a server stream"), because the TV cannot reach the stream the phone makes. Paste Diagnostics from the MKV case.
10. **Stats overlay.** Open it during a remux and during a server conversion (an item over the quality limit, or AirPlay above). Pass: the numbers move twice a second; Transcode shows the server's speed and decisions; taps and double-taps on the picture still work through the panel; Copy gives a light tap and VoiceOver says "Copied"; two columns in landscape, one in portrait; Frames shows no new drops while the panel is open. Screenshot both orientations.
11. **Hardware.** Once per iPhone model: paste the Hardware lines of Diagnostics [H1]; play the HDR files above and the VP9, MPEG-2, VC-1, Hi10P H.264 and AV1 clips, noting which route each takes [H2, H3]; then ten minutes of 4K with Low Power Mode on [H4]. Pass: each clip the Hardware lines call hardware-decodable plays on the phone, and the rest go to the server; with Low Power Mode on, the Heat line says so and playback carries on.
12. **Chromecast.** Turn on Settings › Chromecast, then cast from the player's route popover to a Chromecast (and a Chromecast Ultra or Google TV Streamer if there is one): once a direct-play MP4, once an MKV, once with subtitles on. Pass: the receiver is listed within a few seconds; the MP4 plays as it is and the MKV and the subtitled one stream from the server with the subtitle burned in; play, pause, seek, skip and the Lock Screen control the TV; Skip Intro and Up Next still work; the item is marked watched near the end; This iPhone brings it back paused at the same time; closing the player stops the TV. Untested on hardware so far.
13. **Theme music.** On a show page with a tune, interrupt it with a call or another system audio interruption. Pass: the tune stops and does not restart when the interruption ends; a later page can play its own tune. Switch between servers with matching theme paths and confirm the new server's tune plays. Untested on hardware so far.
14. **Offline downloads.** Download an original file and a server-converted file, including an item with multiple audio and subtitle tracks. Turn on airplane mode and turn Wi-Fi off. Pass: both saved files show picture and sound, seek correctly, and available saved audio and subtitle tracks can be selected and played offline. Record the formats and tracks checked.
15. **Background downloads.** Start a large download, send the app to the Home Screen, and observe byte progress with Continue in Background off and on. Record progress and elapsed time; with the option on, check whether bytes continue growing beyond 30 seconds. iOS may stop background work; force-quit continuation is not required.
16. **Offline watch sync.** Play part of a downloaded item offline, reconnect, and check its resume position in Plex. Separately play another item near the end offline, reconnect, and check that Plex marks it watched. Record both outcomes.
17. **Remote and Settings sheets.** With Home Button selected and the phone at home, open the remote from its button and Settings from the profile button, and close each with its button and with a swipe down; open the remote once from the mini bar. Pass: the two button sheets grow out of their button and shrink back into it, a swipe down included, with no jump and scrolling alive afterwards; a page pushed inside Settings still slides; a download played from Settings opens its player over the sheet and closes back onto it; the mini bar's sheet slides up plain; with Reduce Motion on every sheet slides plain; closing the remote leaves the mini bar's status as it was, with no Offline flash; the close and power buttons sit clear of the sheet's top corners in portrait and of the display corners in landscape. Also close the remote after the phone leaves home with it open, when its button is gone, and note what the dismissal does. Untested on hardware so far.
18. **Video Orientation.** Under each setting of Settings › Video & Playback › Video Orientation, open a film and a live channel with the phone held upright. Pass: Follow Device opens upright and turns with the phone; Start in Landscape opens on its side, stays there for as long as the phone stays upright, and turns with the phone, upright included, once the phone has been on its side; Lock to Landscape stays on its side through every turn; closing the player turns the app upright again. Repeat with the iPhone's Portrait Orientation Lock on, and leave for Picture in Picture and come back under each setting, noting what each does. Untested on hardware so far.
19. **Smooth picture.** On a 120 Hz iPhone, two minutes each of: a 24 fps film with slow pans as Direct Play and as an MKV, a 60 fps file, a live channel read from the tuner and one through Plex. Then scroll Home and a library, and show and hide the controls over a playing picture. Pass: pans move evenly, with no stutter that repeats; the Frames line of Stats for Nerds counts no drops after the start, and the direct live diagnostics line reads "drops 0" and names no underruns; scrolling and the controls' fades stay fluid. Paste Diagnostics for each. Untested on hardware so far.
20. **Away from home.** On cellular only, away from home. Open a title the server converts, close it while it starts and open it again at once, three times. Then start a download with Settings › Downloads › Allow Cellular Downloads off, and continue a large download begun at home after leaving. Pass: every start plays, or ends in an error with Retry, never in "preparing" with no end; the download row says Waiting for Wi-Fi and Download on Cellular starts it; the download begun at home carries on away. Untested on hardware so far.
21. **XR glasses.** Viture Pro glasses over USB-C, Settings › XR Glasses & TV on, When a Display Connects at Ask. Connect them with Marquee in the foreground, answer Use as XR Glasses (the question names the display by size, 1920 × 1080 or as the glasses report), then disconnect and connect again; repeat once with Mirror chosen and once with the plugin off. Pass: the question comes once and the answer is kept; the glasses show Marquee's own screen, not the phone's, with black reading as see-through and the text sharp and clear of the soft edges; the phone becomes the remote on its own, dims, and gets its own brightness back when the remote goes or Marquee leaves the foreground; Home's remote button turns into a glasses button while they are on; Mirror and plugin-off both leave iOS mirroring the phone; the phone's dark theme shows on the glasses and follows a change at once. Play an SDR film, an HDR10 film and a live channel on the glasses, press the volume buttons, then pull the cable mid-film. Pass: picture, subtitles and sound on the glasses; the volume changes and its bar shows; the phone does not lock; after the cable comes out the film is on the phone, paused, where it was. Paste Diagnostics from the HDR film and describe its brightness. Untested on hardware so far.
22. **Eyes-free remote.** With the glasses on and the phone held out of sight, browse Home and a library, open a show, pick an episode, scrub and change subtitles, without looking at the phone. Pass: each swipe moves the focus one tile; a hard flick on a long row coasts on and stops at the end with the end buzz; a tap chooses; a swipe in from the left edge goes back; a hold opens Options; two fingers up or down change the phone's volume a step at a time with the glasses' level bar showing; in the player, hold and slide scrubs with a click every 10 seconds; focus step, end of list, choose, back, scrub detent, play or pause and an error each feel different. With Tilt While Holding on, holding still after a swipe and tilting keeps scrolling, and tilting while scrubbing fine-tunes; picking the phone up, putting it down or walking moves nothing; with the setting off tilt does nothing. With VoiceOver on, the gestures still work, the focused title is read out, and the actions rotor holds Back, Options, Volume Up, Volume Down and Use the Phone App. Untested on hardware so far.
23. **TV on USB-C video out.** An iPhone 15 Pro or later on a TV over USB-C to HDMI or DisplayPort, Settings › XR Glasses & TV on, When a Display Connects at Ask. Connect with Marquee in the foreground. Pass: the question names the TV (its name if the audio route gives one, else its size, such as 3840 × 2160); Use as TV puts Marquee's screen on the TV and the remote on the phone, the TV's symbol on Home's button; the canvas is the theme's dark canvas, not black, with nothing cut off at the TV's edges (compare with the TV's overscan setting; the margin is 5 %), and text sharp at the TV's native resolution (record the size the question shows, and whether iOS gave 1080p or 4K). Unplug and reconnect: no question, the same screen. Settings › Remembered Displays lists the TV as TV; swiping it away makes the next connection ask. Answer Mirror once: the TV mirrors the phone, and replugging stays mirrored. Then connect the glasses and answer Use as XR Glasses: the two keep their own answers. Play an SDR film, an HDR10 film and a Dolby Vision film on an HDR TV and an SDR TV: the picture fills the TV at its resolution, HDR shows as HDR only on the HDR TV (the SDR TV gets a tone-mapped picture), and Diagnostics shows the TV's resolution as the target. Record whether the TV's HDR indicator comes on, what `potentialEDRHeadroom` the TV reports (HDR detection reads it), and whether AirPlay to an Apple TV or TV shows Marquee's screen at all (out of scope; this is only to record what happens). Untested on hardware so far.
24. **Linked phones.** Two iPhones with Marquee on the same Wi-Fi, Settings › Linked Phones on in both. On the first, Show Link Code; on the second, Scan Link Code and confirm. Drive a TV or XR glasses from the first and control it from the second's Home button; then the same with the first playing on its own screen. Then lock and unlock each phone, take one off the Wi-Fi and back, rename one, and unlink on one. Pass: each phone asks for the local network and the camera once; the code reads within two seconds and asks "Link with <name>?" first; both list each other as connected within a few seconds; the second's Home shows Control <name> only while the first drives a big screen or plays; swipes, taps, back and holds act on the first's screen at once and the second's haptics match each answer (end of list, choose, back); the player controls' title, play state and time follow the first within a second, and play, pause, skip, Next and Subtitles act on it; after a lock or a Wi-Fi drop the phones find each other again on their own once both are back in Marquee; a rename shows on the other phone; an unlink removes the link on both while both are in reach. Note the delay from a swipe to its haptic. Untested on hardware so far. Then again with the two phones on different Wi-Fi networks, and with neither joined to any network (Wi-Fi on): they should still find each other. **Volume over HDMI.** With the first on a TV over USB-C to HDMI playing a film, and again on Live TV read from the tuner, open the second's remote and press its volume buttons up and down, swipe two fingers up and down on its screen, and, with the first playing on its own screen, tap − and + on the controls. Pass: each press or 36 pt of swipe changes the TV's loudness by one step (a sixteenth of the range) within half a second, and the TV screen shows the level bar; the second phone's own volume is the same after each press and the same as before the remote opened once Done is tapped; no system volume panel covers either phone's screen; each step has a soft thump on the second phone and silent or full a firmer double thump; the − and + level matches the first phone's own buttons. Note whether the TV's loudness follows the first phone's system level, or only the player's own volume does (the fallback), and the delay from a press to the change. Untested on hardware so far.
25. **Quality downloads.** At home, with Settings › Integrations › HomeLabber Quality Map set up, a film HomeLabber has measured, one it has not and an episode of an animated show. Open each Download menu, then download Medium of the measured film and Low of the other two. After a minute send the app to the Home Screen and lock the phone for ten minutes; then open it, pause one download, force-quit, reopen and resume it. Pass: each menu lists Original, High, Medium and Low with sizes, Measured only for the measured film; each download starts within seconds rather than after Plex's whole conversion, and grows while the phone is locked; the resumed file plays offline from start to end with no jump, freeze or repeat where it resumed; Plex's dashboard shows no conversion left running after each finishes or pauses. Record each file's size beside the menu's and paste Diagnostics.
